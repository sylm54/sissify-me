# Autogynephilia (AGP)

Theory background. The training side lives in `docs/training/autogynephilic.md`.

## Definition

Autogynephilia is a natal male's propensity to experience sexual arousal from the thought or image of himself as a woman, or with female attributes. Ray Blanchard coined the term (1989) and framed it as a paraphilia — in some formulations an "erotic target location error," where the preferred erotic target (women) is internalized so that being female becomes arousing in itself. AGP frequently coexists with ordinary attraction to women, and can compete with or overshadow it. Stronger anatomic forms correlate with higher levels of gender dysphoria (Blanchard, Lawrence).

## Types

Four main, non-exclusive, commonly co-occurring types:

- **Transvestic** — arousal from wearing or fantasizing women's clothing. Most common; overlaps classic transvestic fetishism.
- **Behavioral** — arousal from performing or imagining feminine behaviors, mannerisms, social roles.
- **Physiologic** — arousal from the fantasy or simulation of female bodily functions (menstruation, pregnancy, lactation).
- **Anatomic** — arousal from the thought of possessing female anatomy (breasts, vulva, body shape). Strongest association with dysphoria and interest in physical transformation.

Related distinctions: **partial vs complete** (selected features — e.g. breasts while retaining male genitals — vs a fully female body; complete forms correlate more strongly with dysphoria and surgical interest), and **interpersonal** (arousal from being perceived or interacting as a woman).

## Intensity spectrum

AGP is a continuum, not a binary. Mild: occasional fantasy, transient interest, no distress. Moderate: regular fantasy and enactment, emotional attachment to the feminine self-image. Strong: frequent or dominant AGP arousal competing with external attractions, elevated dysphoria risk. Intensity fluctuates — it can diminish with age, after orgasm, or under competing life demands, and can also hold steady or intensify. Many experience it privately without seeking transition or clinical help.

## Innate orientation, cultivated intensity

The literature treats AGP as an innate sexual orientation or paraphilic propensity emerging around puberty — not something constructed from a neutral baseline. What conditioning *can* do (reported consistently in community practice, consistent with general sexual-conditioning principles): strengthen associative links and raise the salience of AGP arousal through repeated fantasy rehearsal, cross-dressing, erotic material, role-play, and deliberate focus on feminine embodiment. That is exactly what this framework's training does. Suppression attempts show variable success; management and integration are the realistic goals.

## Broadening beyond the erotic

AGP in its erotic core is tightly linked to high-arousal states and fades afterward — which drives oscillation. Broadening it into a stable identity element means building non-erotic associations:

- Regular, scheduled **non-sexual feminine practice** — grooming, clothing during ordinary tasks, posture or voice work — independent of arousal. Over time this creates conditioned comfort separate from the erotic trigger.
- **Journaling non-sexual benefits** — calm, reduced performance pressure, aesthetic pleasure, congruence — reframing "this is only hot when aroused" toward "this improves my state."
- **Low-intensity extension into daily life** — subtle undergarments, skincare, mannerisms — monitored for genuine ease rather than forced performance.
- **Non-erotic rewards** — affirmation of presence, service quality, emotional connection rather than purely erotic validation.

Consistency and patience are the levers. Forcing identity labeling provokes rebound dissonance.

## Oscillation and dissonance

Strong identification alternating with doubt, detachment, or reassertion of a masculine self is a normal feature of AGP-linked trajectories — residual masculine socialization, fluctuating arousal, shame residues, and the partial nature of most cross-gender experience all feed it. Dissonance sounds like "Is this who I am or merely a fetish?", shows up as temporary dysphoria when not expressing femininely, or as post-engagement clarity questioning the prior state.

Management:

- Frame oscillation as **normative** — a continuum of expression, not pathology, and not evidence the desire is fake.
- **Track both poles** — journals recording triggers, intensity, duration, and emotion reveal cycles tied to stress, relationships, or arousal state, enabling anticipatory coping.
- **Maintain a baseline** — a reduced-intensity feminine practice during low periods prevents full disconnection and the rebound urges that follow.
- **Separate erotic intensity from identity value** — the feminine elements retain worth when not currently arousing.
- **Bridge with temporary labels** — "sissy when it feels right" avoids both suppression ("stop forever") and over-identification ("become fully female now").
- **For the agent**: accept both poles consistently. Don't escalate during high-identification phases or shame the low phases. If genuine gender dysphoria emerges as distinct from erotic AGP, support exploration — never push toward either permanent emasculation or suppression.

Where AGP stays primarily erotic, oscillation may never fully resolve; the goal is skill at riding it — engaging when the pull is present, resting without self-recrimination when it recedes, integrating the feminine as one valued aspect of a whole self.

## Pauses vs purges

A non-destructive pause — days or weeks without feminine practice, no destruction — is adaptive regulation, not rejection. It tracks life demands and arousal ebbs, and desire typically resurfaces on its own. Treat pauses as permitted recovery: agree time-limited breaks, keep storage discreet, schedule low-pressure check-ins.

A **purge** is different: discarding items or attempting permanent cessation under acute shame or post-orgasm clarity. For prevention and handling, see `docs/training/tailoring.md`.
